## Freight fraud: what you’re up against

*Lesson 8.1 · The problem*

Lesson 8.1 · The problem

# Freight fraud: what you’re up against

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/theft.jpg)

### Six schemes that hit carriers and dispatchers

| Scheme | How it works | How the carrier loses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Double brokering** | A load is re-sold, often by someone using a stolen carrier or broker identity | Hauls it and never gets paid |
| **Broker impersonation** | Fake emails and phones copy a real broker’s name | Unpaid load, stolen packet, or worse |
| **Carrier identity theft** | Criminals use *your* carrier’s MC/DOT to book loads and steal freight | Claims, police calls, blocked by brokers |
| **Fictitious pickup** | A fake carrier picks up real freight and disappears | Brokers tighten rules; honest carriers get more checks |
| **Phishing** | “Load details” links steal board, email or factoring logins | Account takeover, diverted payments |
| **Payment diversion** | Fake NOA or bank-change letters redirect the broker’s payment | The money goes to the thief |

## How a “normal” load goes unpaid

*Lesson 8.1 · Double brokering*

Lesson 8.1 · Double brokering

# How a “normal” load goes unpaid

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/dbl.jpg)

### Why it works

Everyone in the chain sees something normal: a shipper hands over freight, a truck shows up, the load delivers. The only one who notices is the carrier, weeks later, when the invoice bounces between companies that don’t know each other.

The fix is upstream: **vet the broker before booking** (Lesson 8.2) and **check the BOL at pickup** (page 11).

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Re-brokering is fine if everyone gets paid”**
>
> If you are a carrier (or its dispatcher), you haul the loads you accept. Handing a load to another carrier is brokering. Without broker registration and the $75,000 security it is unlawful, whether or not everyone gets paid.

> **[KEY RULE] One test**
>
> If the shipper’s paperwork, the broker on your rate con and the company that calls you don’t all line up, stop and verify before the truck moves.

### Protect your carrier’s identity

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Lock the FMCSA login** | The owner keeps the FMCSA/Motus login private with multi-factor authentication; thieves change contact details to hijack authority |
| **One official email and phone** | Keep them current on the FMCSA record and tell brokers to trust only those |
| **Use identity platforms** | Setup platforms such as Highway let brokers confirm they are dealing with the real carrier |
| **Watch for strange calls** | A broker asking about a load you never booked = possible identity theft. Tell the carrier the same day |
| **Don’t post documents publicly** | MC certificate, COI and W-9 go only to verified brokers, never to social media groups |

## Real broker or copy?

*Lesson 8.1 · Impersonation*

Lesson 8.1 · Impersonation

# Real broker or copy?

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/emails.jpg)

### Protect your own accounts

- Turn on **multi-factor authentication** for email, load boards and the factoring portal.
- Never open “rate con” or “load details” links from unknown senders. Log in to the platform yourself.
- Never share login codes, even with someone claiming to be “support”.
- Send carrier documents only through setup platforms or verified addresses.

> **[FACT] FMCSA’s advice**
>
> FMCSA says to confirm broker and carrier phone numbers using SAFER, remember that “even insurance certificates can be fraudulent,” and stop if someone asks you to misrepresent your company, proposes blind loads, offers unusually high rates or pressures a quick deal.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Your carrier can be impersonated too**
>
> If a broker calls asking about a load you never booked, someone may be using Blue Line’s MC. Tell Mike immediately. Carriers can warn brokers through setup platforms and report it to FMCSA.

## Twelve red flags

*Lesson 8.1 · Red flags*

Lesson 8.1 · Red flags

# Twelve red flags

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/flags.jpg)

### The “two flags” rule

One flag has innocent explanations: a new broker has to start somewhere, and a hot load can pay well. **Two or more flags together**, like brand-new authority with a gmail address and a rate $300 above the rest, is the classic fraud pattern. Stop and tell the carrier.

| Flags | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Normal checks, book |
| 1 | Verify that point on an official channel |
| 2+ | Stop · tell the carrier · probably pass |

> **[CHECKLIST] Brokers check carriers, too**
>
> Brokers use setup platforms and monitoring services (like the Carrier411 screen on page 3) to vet carriers, looking at authority age, insurance, safety, and reports such as “cancelled after accepting load”. Every cancellation you cause can follow your carrier.

> **[KEY RULE] Stay polite**
>
> “I just need to verify a couple of things before we book” is normal business. Real brokers respect it. Fraudsters push back or disappear.

### Innocent explanation or fraud pattern?

| Flag | Could be innocent | Fraud pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High rate** | Late load, hard pickup, holiday, tight market; the broker explains why | High rate + new broker + pressure; no clear reason |
| **New authority** | New office of an experienced team; normal contacts, platform setup | New + free email + cell only + unusual payment instructions |
| **Pickup change** | Broker updates the rate con and the shipper confirms | Change by phone only; “meet at a truck stop”; new consignee |
| **New bank details** | Carrier switched factors, with an NOA release letter | Email-only change, urgent, different company name on the account |

Lesson 8.2 · The check

# Vetting a broker in seven steps

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/vetting.jpg)

### Where each check happens

| Check | Where |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 Authority, bond | FMCSA SAFER and L&I (free, public) |
| 3 Contact | Broker’s website, SAFER, board profile |
| 4 History | SAFER/L&I dates, board reviews, your notes |
| 5 Credit | DAT credit score and days to pay, factor’s check |
| 6 Tools | Highway for Carriers (free), board profile |
| 7 Rate con | The document itself, against all of the above |

> **[FACT] Highway for Carriers**
>
> Launched in March 2025 and free for carriers. Search a broker by name, MC or DOT to confirm authority, activity and credit standing, and see potential fraud risks. Carriers also use it for broker setups (Module 5).

> **[KEY RULE] Save what you learn**
>
> Keep a simple broker list: name, MC, official phone, email domain, pay speed, notes. It turns 5 minutes into 30 seconds next time.

## Reading a broker’s record

*Lesson 8.2 · FMCSA records*

Lesson 8.2 · FMCSA records

# Reading a broker’s record

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/safer_b.jpg)

### Carrier record vs broker record

| Field | Carrier (Module 5) | Broker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entity type | CARRIER | BROKER |
| Authority | Motor carrier of property | Broker of property |
| Money filing | BIPD insurance ($750,000+) | BMC-84 bond or BMC-85 trust ($75,000) |
| Power units | 1 or more | Usually 0 |

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Some companies are both**
>
> A company can hold carrier and broker authority. Check which one applies to **this load**: the rate con should name it as broker, and the broker authority must be active.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Motus changes (Module 5)**
>
> FMCSA is moving registration into Motus and adding USDOT status suffixes for new registrants. Broker MC numbers still appear on existing records. Always use the live FMCSA site.

## Broker security and unpaid loads

*Lesson 8.2 · The $75,000 rule*

Lesson 8.2 · The $75,000 rule

# Broker security and unpaid loads

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/bond.jpg)

### If a broker doesn’t pay: the carrier’s escalation ladder

| # | Step | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Check the terms** | Rate con payment terms (for example 30 days), quick-pay option, documents required (signed BOL/POD, invoice) |
| 2 | **Resend and confirm** | Resend invoice and POD to the address on the rate con; ask for the expected pay date in writing |
| 3 | **Factor collections** | If the carrier factors, the factor chases payment; non-recourse terms may cover credit failures |
| 4 | **Bond claim** | Carrier files a claim against the broker’s BMC-84 surety or BMC-85 trust (provider listed on L&I) |
| 5 | **Report** | FMCSA National Consumer Complaint Database; board reviews and credit reports |
| 6 | **Legal** | Carrier’s attorney or a collections agency for larger amounts |

You support the carrier with documents and dates. The carrier (or its factor) owns the claim.

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/bolcheck.jpg)

> **[CHECKLIST] Driver’s 60-second pickup check**
>
> Before leaving the dock the driver sends you a photo of the BOL. You compare: shipper, consignee, commodity, pieces, weight, seal, reference numbers, and **which carrier and broker the shipper expects**.

### What the driver confirms before the truck moves

| Item | Check against | If different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shipper name and address | Rate con pickup | Stop; call the broker |

## The six-step response

*Lesson 8.3 · When something is wrong*

Lesson 8.3 · When something is wrong

# The six-step response

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/respond.jpg)

### Example: the lookalike email

A “Lakeshore Logistics” rep emails from **lakeshore-logistics-llc.com** asking for your packet. You **stop**, **verify** by calling Lakeshore’s number from its website (they have no such rep), **tell Mike**, **protect** (no documents were sent), **report** to Lakeshore, the load board and FMCSA, and **record** the domain on your do-not-use list.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Don’t play detective**
>
> Don’t confront, threaten or “trap” a suspected fraudster. Stop the transaction, protect the carrier, and report to the proper channels.

> **[KEY RULE] Write it down immediately**
>
> Times, numbers, emails, screenshots. Reports and claims go faster with evidence, and memory fades by the next shift.

### Who to tell, and when

| Who | When |
| --- | --- |
| **The carrier (Mike)** | Always, first, in writing |
| **The real broker** | Someone is impersonating them, or their load is being re-sold |
| **The load board** | Fake postings, fake accounts, phishing through the board |
| **The factor** | Payment redirection, fake NOA, suspicious broker credit |
| **FMCSA NCCDB** | Identity theft, unauthorized brokering, broker non-payment |
| **FBI IC3 / FTC / police** | Money lost, cargo stolen, cyber fraud (the carrier files; you supply evidence) |

## Stay on the carrier’s side

*Lesson 8.3 · Your legal lines*

Lesson 8.3 · Your legal lines

# Stay on the carrier’s side

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/legal.jpg)

### How to introduce yourself honestly

**“Hi, this is Jawad, dispatcher for Blue Line Transport, MC 1298456.”** If asked where you are: **“I work remotely for Blue Line. Mike, the owner, can be reached at the number on our FMCSA record.”**

Being honest about your role costs you nothing with good brokers and protects your carrier if anyone checks.

> **[FACT] The law in one line**
>
> A person may provide interstate brokerage only if registered as a broker with FMCSA and meeting the financial security requirement (49 U.S.C. 14916). Penalties can reach $10,000 per violation, and officers and principals can be personally liable.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Dispatchers can share loads between their carriers”**
>
> If you dispatch for two carriers and move a load booked by one to the other, you are acting as a broker. Each carrier books and hauls its own loads.

## Prepare before you dial

*Lesson 8.4 · Negotiation*

Lesson 8.4 · Negotiation

# Prepare before you dial

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/prep.jpg)

### Five principles

1. **Facts first, rate second.** Confirm the load details before you talk money (drill 8).
2. **Know your numbers.** Target, walk-away, market range. Say them in dollars.
3. **Anchor with a reason.** Your first number sets the range. Tie it to miles, market or timing.
4. **Move in shrinking steps.** Big first step, smaller after. It signals you are near your limit.
5. **Be ready to walk.** Politely, with an open door: “If it changes, call me.”

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Always accept the posted rate to win loads”**
>
> Posted rates are often an opening number. Brokers expect carriers to counter, and many lanes have room. Accepting every first offer trains brokers to lowball your carrier.

> **[KEY RULE] Carrier-side, not personal**
>
> You aren’t fighting the broker. You both want the load covered. Your job is to get a fair price for your carrier’s truck, and a respectful tone gets better rates over time.

## Broker lines and calm answers

*Lesson 8.4 · Tactics*

Lesson 8.4 · Tactics

# Broker lines and calm answers

![](/assets/diagrams/m08/lines.jpg)

### Things to trade besides the rate

| Ask | Why it’s worth money |
| --- | --- |
| **Detention terms** | Paid waiting after a set free time (Module 10) |
| **Lumper paid by broker** | Unloading fees can be $100+ |
| **Quick pay terms** | Cash faster, lower or no factoring fee |
| **Extra stop pay** | Each stop costs time and hours |
| **Later pickup / wider window** | Lets the truck finish or rest properly |
| **Backhaul or next load** | Solves the reload problem (Module 7) |

> **[KEY RULE] Silence is a tool**
>
> After you say your number, stop. The first person to fill the silence usually moves. Count to five in your head.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Never bluff about facts**
>
> Don’t invent another offer, a fake appointment or a false truck location. Brokers talk to each other, and one lie costs your carrier more than any single load.

## Closing the deal cleanly

*Lesson 8.4 · Closing*

Lesson 8.4 · Closing

# Closing the deal cleanly

### Before you say “send the rate con”

1. **Total, all-in:** “So that’s $1,750 all-in, including fuel?”
2. **Appointments:** pickup and delivery times, FCFS or appointment, time zone.
3. **Details:** commodity, weight, pieces, equipment, tracking app.
4. **Accessorials:** detention after how many hours and at what rate, lumper, TONU.
5. **Payment:** terms, quick pay, where to send invoices.
6. **Carrier approval:** Mike says yes (or the load is within his written limits).
7. **Setup:** broker has (or will get) the packet through its platform.

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] The close**
>
> **“Mike confirmed. We’ll take it at $1,750 all-in. Detention after two hours, and lumper covered by you, correct? Please send the rate confirmation to our dispatch email and the setup link if you need one. Thank you!”**

> **[CHECKLIST] When the rate con arrives**
>
> Read it line by line **before** anyone signs: rate, all-in, dates, addresses, commodity, weight, accessorials, deductions, payment terms, broker name and MC (Module 9 goes deeper). If anything differs from the call, ask for a corrected rate con.

> **[KEY RULE] Walking away well**
>
> **“I understand. I can’t do it under $1,850 today. If it’s still open later, call me at this number.”** Many loads come back an hour later at your price.

### Five negotiation mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| Asking “what’s your best?” then accepting it | Counter with your number and a reason |
| Negotiating per mile on loaded miles | Talk totals; you know your total-mile math |
| Splitting the difference too early | Shrinking steps: $2,000 → $1,800 → final |
| Agreeing, then “forgetting” detention and lumper | Confirm accessorials before the rate con |
| Booking before the carrier approves | “Let me confirm with my carrier: five minutes.” |

# Four negotiation role-plays

Prepare each with the prep card (page 14): numbers, alternatives, leverage, non-rate asks. Then practise out loud, and when ready, ask Claude to play the broker.

#### Role-play 1 Posted load, room to push

Load 1 · Chicago → Charlotte · posted $2,050 · 760 loaded + 40 DH · practice lane range $2.45–$2.85 per loaded mile ($1,862–$2,166)

**Your numbers:** Target $2,160 · walk-away $2,072 · anchor about $2,250

#### Role-play 2 Broker calls on your posted truck

Joliet → Nashville · 470 loaded + 15 DH · broker offers $1,150 ($2.37 per total mile) · pickup today 2 PM

**Your numbers:** Target $1,310 (485 × $2.70) · ask $1,400 · walk-away $1,256

#### Role-play 3 Rich rate, weak destination

Load 7 · Kankakee → Jacksonville · $2,700 · 1,085 total mi · reloads out of Jacksonville are thin and cheap

**Your numbers:** Ask $2,930+ or pass · use the reload problem as your reason

#### Role-play 4 Slow facility, protect the driver

A load you like at a fair rate, but the shipper is known for 4-hour waits

**Your numbers:** Keep the rate · negotiate detention after 2 h in writing (practice: $50/h) and a later pickup window

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] How to start a role-play with me**

> Type: **“Role-play 2. You’re the broker. I’m Jawad from Blue Line.”** I’ll stay in character, push back realistically, and give you a score and feedback at the end.

> **[CHECKLIST] Score yourself**
>
> Facts before rate? A number with a reason? Shrinking steps? Stayed at or above walk-away? Confirmed all-in and accessorials? Polite close either way?

# Answers: module check

**1.**

Re-selling a load you took as a carrier, often via stolen identities. The real broker pays whoever it contracted with (the fraudster), who never pays your carrier.

**2.**

Any four: rate far above market; brand-new authority; free or lookalike email; cell-only; pressure; hide your identity; new bank details; asks for logins; pickup changes; names don’t match; odd equipment; asks you to pay.

**3.**

SAFER (entity type BROKER, broker authority active) and L&I (BMC-84 surety bond or BMC-85 trust fund, $75,000, no cancellation).

**4.**

The provider notifies FMCSA; FMCSA notifies the broker, who has 7 business days to restore it or show an error; otherwise its authority is suspended.

**5.**

Find the broker’s phone yourself (website, SAFER, board profile), call, and ask for the person by name. Compare the email domain with the website.

**6.**

“Don’t leave yet.” Call the broker on its official number (and the shipper if needed), tell Mike, and move only when it’s resolved in writing.

**7.**

FMCSA NCCDB (nccdb.fmcsa.dot.gov), FMCSA Contact Center 1-800-832-5660, FTC, FBI IC3 or state attorney general, DOT OIG hotline.

**8.**

It is brokering: arranging transport by another carrier for compensation requires broker registration and $75,000 security (49 U.S.C. 14916).

**9.**

The lowest rate you’ll accept: usually break-even × total miles (Module 7), lower only when the carrier agrees for a strategic reason such as a strong reload.

**10.**

Open with a number backed by facts. “For 727 miles with a same-day pickup I need $2,000. The lane has been around $2.60 a mile.”

**11.**

Detention terms, lumper paid, quick pay, stop pay, pickup window, a backhaul.

**12.**

All-in total, appointments, commodity and weight, accessorials, payment terms, carrier approval, setup.

## Glossary

Reference

# Glossary

**BMC-84** — Broker’s $75,000 surety bond filing.

**BMC-85** — Broker’s $75,000 trust fund filing.

**BMC-36** — Notice cancelling a bond or trust.

**Anchor** — First number that sets the negotiation range.

**Blind load** — Shipment where the parties’ identities are hidden; can be legitimate, but a red flag if you’re asked to misrepresent yourself.

**Bond claim** — Carrier’s claim against a broker’s surety or trust for unpaid freight.

**Double brokering** — Re-selling a load taken as a carrier, without authority or disclosure.

**Fictitious pickup** — Theft where a fake carrier collects real freight.

**Highway for Carriers** — Free tool for carriers to verify brokers.

**Identity theft** — Using another company’s MC/DOT or name.

**Impersonation** — Posing as a real broker with lookalike contacts.

**L&I** — FMCSA Licensing & Insurance: authority and filings.

**NCCDB** — FMCSA’s National Consumer Complaint Database.

**Phishing** — Fake links or messages that steal logins.

**SAFER** — FMCSA company snapshot: entity type, status, contacts.

**Target** — The rate you aim for: target × total miles.

**Walk-away** — Lowest acceptable rate; usually break-even.

**Unauthorized brokering** — Arranging carriage without broker registration and security; unlawful.
