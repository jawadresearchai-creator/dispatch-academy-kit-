## A marketplace, not a contract

*Lesson 6.1 · How it works*

Lesson 6.1 · How it works

# A marketplace, not a contract

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/market.jpg)

### Who uses load boards

- **Brokers** post loads they need trucks for, and search posted trucks.
- **Carriers and dispatchers** search loads and post available trucks.
- Boards add tools: **rate data**, **broker credit and days-to-pay**, reviews, alerts, “book now”.

The biggest U.S. boards are **DAT** and **Truckstop**; others include 123Loadboard. Big brokers also run their own apps and portals.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] A posting is not a promise**
>
> Postings can be old, already covered, missing details, or fake (Module 8). Rates can change on the phone. **Nothing is booked until the carrier signs a rate confirmation.**

> **[FACT] Access reminder (Module 0)**
>
> DAT lets carriers with authority add a **seat for their dispatcher**. Truckstop requires active carrier authority to search loads. Never borrow logins.

### Where a carrier’s loads come from

| Source | How it works | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Load boards** | Search posted loads, call or email the broker, or book instantly where offered | New carriers; finding freight anywhere, fast |
| **Truck postings** | Post the empty truck; brokers call you | Thin markets; slow nights |
| **Repeat brokers** | Brokers you have hauled for email their lanes or call first | Better rates and trust over time |
| **Broker apps / portals** | Large brokers let set-up carriers book loads inside their own app | After the carrier is onboarded with that broker (Module 5) |
| **Direct shippers** | The carrier contracts with a shipper directly | Established carriers; not your first month |

# Four real boards, one logic

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/gallery.jpg)
> **[KEY RULE] Learn the columns, not the colours**
>
> Every board shows the same core facts: **age · pickup date · equipment · origin + deadhead · destination · trip miles · length/weight · company · credit/pay signals · rate**. Once you can read one, you can read them all, even after a redesign.

#### DAT One

The largest U.S. board. Broker **credit score** and **days to pay**, market rates (RateView), truck postings with alarms, TriHaul route suggestions (higher plans).

#### Truckstop

Needs active carrier authority to search. **Bond backing**, **RMIS** tags, **Book It Now** loads, rate insights and backhaul suggestions.

#### Others

123Loadboard (in your manual) and similar boards, plus brokers’ own apps. Fewer loads on some, but the same columns and the same screening logic.

Features and plan names change often. Check the board’s own site before telling a carrier what a plan includes; never quote prices from memory.

## The search form

*Lesson 6.2 · Searching*

Lesson 6.2 · Searching

# The search form

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/form.jpg)

### DAT’s definitions (help centre)

- **Origin** — “where your truck is or will be.”
- **DH-O** — “how far you are willing to drive to get a load.”
- **Destination** — city, state or zones; “leaving it blank is the equivalent to ‘anywhere’.”
- **Weight** — “the maximum cargo weight you can haul.”
- **Search back** — shows loads posted or refreshed in the last X hours.

> **[KEY RULE] Fill it from facts**
>
> Weight = the truck’s confirmed legal max (Module 2). Dates = when the driver is really empty and legal (Module 4). Equipment = what the truck really is. A search built on wishes finds loads you can’t haul.

> **[TRY IT] Try it**
>
> Truck 12 prefers the Southeast. Which destination would you type: blank, “Charlotte, NC”, or “Z3, Z2”? What changes in the results? (Answer at the back.)

### Five search mistakes beginners make

| Mistake | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- |
| **Weight left at the default** | You see loads the truck can’t legally carry and waste calls |
| **Wrong date** | Loads pick up before the driver is empty or legal to drive |
| **“VR” or “any van” for a plain van** | Reefer-only and special loads fill your screen |
| **Huge DH-O from a big market** | Hundreds of far-away loads bury the close ones |
| **Search back of 24 h** | You call on old postings that were covered hours ago |

## Deadhead circles and zones

*Lesson 6.2 · Deadhead*

Lesson 6.2 · Deadhead

# Deadhead circles and zones

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/dhmap.jpg)

### Choosing the circle size

| Situation | DH-O |
| --- | --- |
| Big freight market (Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta) | 25–75 mi |
| Medium city, decent freight | 75–100 mi |
| Rural area / thin board | 100–150+ mi |

Practice guidelines, not rules. The carrier decides how much deadhead it accepts.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Deadhead is unpaid but not free**
>
> Every empty mile costs fuel, driver pay, tyres and hours (Module 4). A load 90 miles away uses nearly 2 hours of the driver’s clock before loading even starts.

> **[FACT] Zones**
>
> A destination of **Z3** covers AL, FL, GA, MS and TN in one search (Module 3). Type several: **Z3, Z2**.

## Open a load before you call

*Lesson 6.2 · Load details*

Lesson 6.2 · Load details

# Open a load before you call

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/detail.jpg)

### The 20-second pre-call check

1. **Fit:** equipment, length, weight, commodity.
2. **Time:** pickup window vs truck availability; delivery if shown.
3. **Lane:** direction, zones, next-load market.
4. **Money:** posted rate ÷ total miles vs carrier minimum.
5. **Broker:** credit score, days to pay, reviews, MC.

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Say it out loud — drill 8: the first 15 seconds**
>
> **“Hi, this is Jawad with Blue Line Transport, MC 1298456. I’m calling about your load LL-448210, Chicago to Charlotte, picking up Wednesday. Is it still available?”**  
>   
> If yes: **“Great. What’s the commodity and weight, and what are the pickup and delivery appointments?”** Rate comes after the facts (Module 8).

## Will this broker pay — and how fast?

*Lesson 6.3 · Broker signals*

Lesson 6.3 · Broker signals

# Will this broker pay — and how fast?

### DAT signals

| Signal | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Credit score (CS)** | DAT’s credit rating for the company, calculated with Equifax from days-to-pay trends, collections, bankruptcy alerts and other data. Higher is better; new brokers may have no score. |
| **Days to pay (DTP)** | Average days the company takes to pay invoices. Around 30 is normal; 45–60+ is slow. |
| **Reviews** | Star ratings from carriers. |

### Truckstop signals

| Signal | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Bond backing** | Shows the broker’s surety bond status |
| **RMIS verification** | Broker uses RMIS onboarding |
| **Factorable / new / private** | Load tags: can the carrier’s factor buy this invoice? |
| **Rate insights** | Pricing context before you call |

**Real Truckstop search** (your manual): Charlotte → anywhere and Ontario, CA → AZ, with rate, Book It Now, company, O-DH/D-DH, distance and weight columns.

> **[KEY RULE] Ask your factoring company**
>
> If the carrier factors, the factor may refuse to buy invoices from low-credit brokers. Many factors offer a **broker credit check**. Check it before booking a load from an unfamiliar broker.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Signals are not verification**
>
> A good score doesn’t prove the person on the phone really works for that broker. Identity theft and double brokering are covered in Module 8.

## Posting trucks and alarms

*Lesson 6.4 · Posting*

Lesson 6.4 · Posting

# Posting trucks and alarms

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/post.jpg)

### When to post

- As soon as you know when and where the truck will be empty, ideally the day before.
- Post each truck separately with its real details.
- Update the posting if the time or location changes; remove it when booked.

> **[FACT] What happens next**
>
> Brokers searching for trucks see your posting and call or email. Be ready to answer: the truck’s location, availability, equipment, and a rate. Keep the carrier’s minimum in mind (Module 7).

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] When a broker calls about your posting**
>
> **“Blue Line Transport, this is Jawad.”** Broker: “I see your truck in Joliet. I have a load to Atlanta.”  
> **“Sure. What’s the pickup location and time, the delivery appointment, the commodity and weight?”** Then: **“What are you paying on it?”** If it works, get the load details in writing before committing.

|  | Searching loads | Posting trucks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who calls** | You call the broker | The broker calls you |
| **Speed** | Fast when the board is busy | Slower; works while you do other things |
| **Use it when** | Always, first | Thin markets, odd equipment, late nights, and always as a backup |

## A search routine and good habits

*Lesson 6.4 · Discipline*

Lesson 6.4 · Discipline

# A search routine and good habits

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/routine.jpg)

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **One saved search per truck** | Refresh with one click; alarms work per search or posting |
| **Short search-back (2–4 h)** | Old postings are often covered; fresh ones get answered |
| **Sort by age or pickup** | See new loads first; don’t scroll randomly |
| **Blank destination, then zones** | See everything, then narrow to the carrier’s preferred regions |
| **Note every call** | Load ID, broker, contact, offer, answer: you will call again |
| **Widen slowly** | Increase DH-O or zones only if the board is thin |

> **[FACT] Your clock vs their clock**
>
> When it is **8:00 AM in Chicago (CT)**, it is **6:00 PM in Pakistan** during U.S. daylight time (March–November) and **7:00 PM** in winter. Many brokers post fresh loads at the start of their business day, so the first two hours of your shift matter most.

> **[KEY RULE] Friday rule of thumb**
>
> On Friday, brokers post loads for Monday pickup. If a truck will be empty over the weekend, search Monday pickups on Friday and ask the carrier if the driver is taking a weekend break.

## The funnel: screen fast, call few

*Lesson 6.4 · Screening*

Lesson 6.4 · Screening

# The funnel: screen fast, call few

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/funnel.jpg)

### The five-filter scan (per load, ~10 seconds)

1. **Can we haul it?** Equipment, weight, length, hazmat.
2. **Can we get there?** Deadhead, pickup date and time, hours.
3. **Can we deliver it?** Appointment feasible (Module 4).
4. **Does it go somewhere good?** Lane, carrier preference, next load.
5. **Is it worth calling?** Rate per total mile, broker signals.

> **[KEY RULE] Call in order**
>
> Call the best load first, but have the second and third ready. If the first is gone, you lose seconds, not the evening.

### Worked example: three loads through the five filters

| Filter | Load 1 · Chicago → Charlotte | Load 5 · Aurora → Memphis | Load 11 · Chicago → Birmingham |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 Haul it?** | V, 53 ft, 42,000 lb OK | 46,500 lb > 44,500 STOP | VA = air-ride required STOP |
| **2 Get there?** | 40 mi DH, Wed OK | — | — |
| **3 Deliver it?** | Thu FCFS 8–4 ET OK | — | — |
| **4 Good lane?** | Southeast OK | — | — |
| **5 Worth calling?** | $2.56/total mi, CS 96 CALL | — | — |

Stop at the first failed filter. Two of these three loads take two seconds each.

## Backhauls and triangles

*Lesson 6.4 · Beyond one load*

Lesson 6.4 · Beyond one load

# Backhauls and triangles

![](/assets/diagrams/m06/triangle.jpg)

### Search the next leg first

Before booking Joliet → Charlotte, run a quick search: **Charlotte (DH-O 75) → Z4, Z6** for Thursday or Friday. Many decent loads back toward the Midwest means the truck is well placed. Few or cheap loads means you should value the outbound load lower, or pick a different destination.

> **[FACT] Tools that help**
>
> DAT’s guide describes **TriHaul** (DAT One Pro), which suggests triangular routes to reduce deadhead. Truckstop highlights backhauls to reduce deadhead. Rate history tools (DAT RateView) come in Module 7.

### Headhaul, backhaul, triangle

| Term | Meaning | For Truck 12 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Headhaul** | A lane with lots of freight and fewer trucks: rates are usually stronger | Depends on the market that week: check, don’t assume |
| **Backhaul** | The return direction, often with more trucks than loads: rates are usually weaker | A load back toward Joliet after the Southeast run |
| **Triangle** | Three legs instead of an out-and-back, to avoid a weak return lane | Joliet → Charlotte → Atlanta → Midwest |

> **[TRY IT] Try it**
>
> Truck 12 books Load 7 to Jacksonville, FL. Write the next search. (Suggested: origin Jacksonville, DH-O 75, destination Z4, Z6 or blank, van, full, 53 ft, 44,500 lb, delivery day + 1. Check the load count before booking the outbound leg.)

## Glossary

*Reference*

Reference

# Glossary

**Age** — Time since a posting was created or refreshed.

**Alarm / alert** — Notification when new matching loads appear.

**Backhaul** — Load that brings the truck back toward home or a good market.

**Book now** — Book online without a call; still produces a rate confirmation.

**Credit score (CS)** — DAT broker credit rating.

**DH-O / DH-D** — Deadhead to origin / from destination.

**Days to pay (DTP)** — Average payment time.

**Factorable** — Load whose invoice a factor will buy.

**F / P** — Full or partial load.

**Load board** — Online marketplace of loads and trucks.

**Post a truck** — Advertise available capacity.

**Private loads** — Loads visible only to selected carriers.

**Rate per mile** — Rate ÷ miles (loaded, or total including deadhead).

**Reference ID** — The broker’s load number on the board.

**Saved search** — Stored search criteria for quick refresh.

**Search back** — Only show loads posted in the last X hours.

**Trip** — Loaded miles from pickup to delivery.

**TriHaul** — DAT tool suggesting triangular routes.

**Zone** — Region code (Z0–Z9) for searches.

### Board codes you’ll see (DAT)

| Code | Meaning | Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **V** | Van (dry van) | **F** | Flatbed |
| **VA** | Van with air-ride | **SD** | Step deck |
| **VM** | Van with team | **R** | Reefer |
| **VC** | Van with curtains (curtainside) | **VR** | Van or reefer: either can haul it |
| **MV** | Moving van | **F / P** | Full or partial load |

Codes follow Module 2. Boards add and rename types; hover or open the equipment list on the board to confirm.
