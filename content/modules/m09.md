## The clauses that cost money

*Lesson 9.1 · Real rate con*

Lesson 9.1 · Real rate con

# The clauses that cost money

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/rc_terms.jpg)

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “The rate con is just a receipt for the rate”**
>
> It is the contract for this load. It usually refers to the broker–carrier agreement signed at setup, and together they set the rules: payment, fines, liability and accessorials.

> **[FACT] Right to see the record**
>
> FMCSA requires brokers to keep a record of each brokered transaction for three years, and **each party to the transaction has the right to review it** (49 CFR 371.3).

### Common rate con clauses and how to respond

| Clause | What it means | Your move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tracking required** | Deduction if tracking is refused or stops | Set up tracking before pickup; tell the driver |
| **POD deadline** | Fine if the POD arrives late (e.g. 72 h) | Send the POD the same day |
| **Late delivery fine** | Money off if late, sometimes a fixed amount | Ask for a grace period, or removal when the delay isn’t the carrier’s fault |
| **Detention terms** | Free time, hourly rate, cap, notice required | Know the notice rule (e.g. call when 2 h reached) and log in/out times |
| **TONU** | Pay if the load cancels on arrival, or “not liable” | Ask for TONU on loads with long deadhead |
| **No re-brokering** | Carrier must haul the load itself | Always true for you (Module 8) |
| **Cargo liability** | Carrier responsible for loss, damage, shortage | Check the carrier’s cargo insurance limits cover the freight value |

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] When something is wrong**
>
> **“Hi, this is Jawad with Blue Line, about load PF-20931. The rate con shows $1,650 and a reefer. We agreed $1,750 for a dry van. Could you send a corrected rate con, please?”**

> **[KEY RULE] Fix it before anyone signs**
>
> Never sign “and fix it later”. Once signed, the wrong rate or missing detention is what the carrier agreed to. Ask for a corrected version; check it again.

> **[CHECKLIST] After signing**
>
> Save the signed copy in the load folder (page 15), send the setup documents if requested, and only then send the dispatch sheet to the driver.

Lesson 9.2 · Dispatch

# Briefing the driver

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/dsheet.jpg)
> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Confirm by phone**
>
> **“Carlos, you’re set for PF-20931: Bolingbrook, pickup number 5-5-8-1-2, window 9 to 11 Central tomorrow. Delivery Atlanta Thursday 1 PM Eastern. Can you read back the pickup number and the appointment?”**

### Before the driver rolls

- Signed rate con saved; setup complete with the broker.
- Dispatch sheet sent and read back by the driver.
- Tracking link sent and accepted (or ELD tracking connected).
- Hours checked: the plan fits the driver’s clocks (Module 4).
- Route checked: truck entrance, parking, any low bridges or restricted roads.
- Broker has the driver’s name, truck and trailer numbers, and cell if required.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] If the driver says the plan won’t work**
>
> Listen. The driver sees hours, weather and fatigue you don’t. Adjust the plan, tell the broker early, and never push him to drive over his hours (Module 4, coercion is prohibited).

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Driver information sharing**
>
> Brokers often ask for the driver’s name and number for tracking and check calls. Share only what the carrier approves, through the broker’s official channel.

## At the shipper

*Lesson 9.3 · Pickup*

Lesson 9.3 · Pickup

# At the shipper

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/pickup.jpg)

### Live load vs drop and hook

| Type | What happens | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live load** | Driver waits while the shipper loads | In/out times for detention |
| **Drop and hook** | Driver drops an empty trailer, takes a pre-loaded one | Seal, SLC, trailer number, condition |
| **Preloaded** | Trailer loaded before arrival | SLC, seal photo, weight |

> **[FACT] The law behind SLC**
>
> When the bill of lading shows the shipper loaded the goods, federal law (49 U.S.C. 80113) limits the carrier’s liability for damage caused by improper loading. The driver still must refuse a load that is visibly unsafe.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Overweight or unsafe load?**
>
> If the load looks overweight, poorly secured or not as described, the driver doesn’t leave. Scale it if needed (Module 2) and call the broker. Safety comes before the appointment.

# Check calls and tracking

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/chat.jpg)

### Tracking apps

Brokers track loads with tools such as **Descartes MacroPoint** or **Trucker Tools**. MacroPoint can pull location from the carrier’s **ELD** (preferred: no app needed), from a TMS, or from a driver phone app. It says it tracks only during the load session.

The rate con for #12354 deducted **$150** if the driver refused or disabled tracking. Make sure it is running before the truck leaves.

> **[KEY RULE] Check-call rhythm**
>
> At dispatch · at shipper · loaded (BOL photo) · every 3–4 hours or at breaks · any delay, immediately · at receiver · empty (POD photo).

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Pakistan tip**
>
> Use one group chat per truck with the carrier included, plus a pinned message with the current load. Everyone sees the same information, and nothing depends on your shift.

## Three documents, one story

*Lesson 9.4 · The paper trail*

Lesson 9.4 · The paper trail

# Three documents, one story

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/chain.jpg)

### Spot the mismatch

Compare #12354’s rate con and BOL: rate con weight **2,000 lb**, BOL weight **5,729 lb** (Module 8, page 11). The POD shows 10 units received, matching the BOL. A broker AP team may ask about the weight difference before paying. That’s why you resolve mismatches at pickup, in writing.

> **[FACT] What a BOL must include**
>
> Under 49 CFR 373.101: consignor and consignee names; origin and destination; number of packages; description of the freight; weight, volume or measurement when used for rating.

### What each document proves

| Document | Proves | Issued by | Carrier keeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rate con** | The price and rules agreed | Broker | Signed copy |
| **BOL** | What was handed to the carrier, and in what condition | Shipper | Driver’s copy + photo |
| **POD** | What the receiver accepted, and any exceptions | Receiver signs the BOL/receipt | Signed copy |
| **Receipts** | Costs paid on the broker’s behalf (lumper, scale) | Facility / vendor | Original + scan |
| **Invoice** | What the carrier asks to be paid | Carrier (you prepare) | Copy + sent email |

## The invoice

*Lesson 9.4 · Invoicing*

Lesson 9.4 · Invoicing

# The invoice

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/invoice.jpg)

### Invoice packet checklist

- Invoice with its own number, load #, BOL #, dates, amounts
- Signed rate con (or corrected version)
- Signed BOL/POD, readable
- Receipts for any reimbursed costs (lumper, scale)
- Detention proof: in/out times and broker approval (Module 10)

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Deadlines are money**
>
> Some rate cons set a POD deadline (72 hours on #12354) and fine or deduct after it. Send the packet the same day the truck empties.

> **[KEY RULE] One email, one load**
>
> Subject: “Invoice BL-1024 · Load PF-20931 · POD attached”. Attach PDFs, not links. Save the sent email in the load folder.

### Why invoices get held, and the fix

| Reason | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| POD unreadable or missing a page | Ask the driver to rescan flat, full page; send PDF |
| Amount doesn’t match the rate con | Invoice only what’s on the (corrected) rate con, plus approved extras |
| Wrong load or reference number | Copy the broker’s load # exactly from the rate con |
| Sent to the wrong address | Use the AP email on the rate con, or the factor if factored |
| Detention added without proof or approval | Attach in/out times and the broker’s written approval (Module 10) |

# Keeping the records

![](/assets/diagrams/m09/files.jpg)

### Close-out checklist (after payment)

1. Payment amount matches the invoice (or the deduction is explained).
2. Payment confirmation saved in the load folder.
3. Invoice tracker marked paid.
4. Broker notes updated: pay speed, service, issues.
5. Load economics card updated with the real result (Module 7).

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Privacy**
>
> Carrier documents contain bank details, tax IDs and driver information. Keep them in the carrier’s system only, with a strong password and MFA. Never forward them from personal accounts.

### Photo and scan standards for drivers

| Good | Bad |
| --- | --- |
| Whole page, flat, all four corners visible | Cut-off edges, folded page, finger over text |
| Daylight or cab light, no shadow | Dark cab, flash glare on the stamp |
| Scanner app → one PDF, all pages | Five separate blurry photos |
| Signature, stamp and count readable | “It was signed, trust me” |

## Glossary

*Reference*

Reference

# Glossary

**BOL** — Bill of lading: receipt and contract for the shipment.

**Check call** — Status update from the driver or to the broker.

**Clean POD** — Signed delivery with no shortage or damage noted.

**Dispatch sheet** — The driver’s load instructions.

**Drop and hook** — Swap trailers instead of waiting to load.

**Exception** — Shortage, overage, damage or refusal noted on the POD.

**FCFS** — First come, first served: no fixed appointment.

**Invoice packet** — Invoice + rate con + POD + receipts.

**Live load/unload** — Driver waits while freight is loaded or unloaded.

**OS&D** — Over, short and damaged.

**POD** — Proof of delivery: the signed BOL or delivery receipt.

**PU# / DEL#** — Pickup or delivery reference numbers.

**Quick pay** — Broker pays early for a fee.

**Rate con** — Rate confirmation: the load contract.

**Seal** — Numbered tag locking the trailer doors.

**SLC** — Shipper load and count.

**TONU** — Truck ordered, not used: pay if a load cancels on arrival.

**Tracking** — Location sharing with the broker (ELD or app).
