# How to use this module

Module 9 showed Load 2 going perfectly. Real weeks don’t. Receivers make trucks wait, loads cancel at the gate, highways close, trucks break, freight arrives crushed. This module teaches you to **protect the driver, the schedule and the carrier’s money** when that happens.

### Practice terms used in this module

#### Blue Line’s usual asks (practice values)

Detention **2 h free, then $50/h** · layover **$250/day** · TONU **$150** · extra stop **$75** · driver assist **$75** (only if agreed) · lumper **paid or reimbursed by broker**.

Real terms differ by broker and load. The rate con always decides (Module 9).

### Study rhythm (about 5 hours)

- **Session 1 (1.5 h):** accessorials, detention clock, lumpers, TONU and layover.
- **Session 2 (1.5 h):** problems on the road, late ETA call (drill 12), breakdowns, accidents.
- **Session 3 (2 h):** claims, evidence kit, practice situations and check.

Hours of waiting are hours of pay,  
if you can prove them.

ATRI found drivers were detained at 39% of stops in 2023, and fleets were paid for fewer than half of the detention invoices they sent. The difference between paid and unpaid is almost always the same: times, notice and proof.

# The accessorials menu

### Three conditions for getting paid

1. **Agreed:** on the rate con, or approved by the broker in writing before or during the event.
2. **Proven:** times, receipts, messages, photos (evidence kit, page 14).
3. **Invoiced properly:** a separate line on the invoice, with the proof attached, sent with the POD.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Accessorials are automatic”**
>
> Brokers pay what the rate con says and what you can prove. A driver who waited four hours without anyone telling the broker usually gets nothing.

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Ask at booking**
>
> **“What are your detention terms: free time, rate, and how do you want to be notified?”** Then check the answer on the rate con.

## The detention clock

*Lesson 10.1 · Detention*

Lesson 10.1 · Detention

# The detention clock

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/dclock.jpg)

### Your detention routine

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | Driver checks in; photo of the check-in sheet or gate time; tracking shows arrival |
| 15 min before free time ends | Email/message the broker: “Driver on site since 12:00 for 1 PM appt, not yet in a door. Free time ends 3 PM; please notify the receiver and approve detention after 3.” |
| Departure | In and out times written on the BOL/POD and signed by the facility |
| Same day | Detention line on the invoice with times and the notice message attached |

> **[FACT] Hours of service still apply**
>
> Waiting at a dock is usually on-duty time and uses the 14-hour window (Module 4). Long detention can push a driver out of hours, which is why detention can also mean a missed next load.

> **[KEY RULE] Know the fine print**
>
> Some brokers pay only if the driver arrived on time, only after written notice, or only up to a daily cap. Ask once, write it down for that broker, and follow it every time.

### The detention notice (template)

**Subject:** Detention notice · Load PF-20931 · Atlanta  
  
Hi Dana, our driver Carlos arrived at 12:00 PM ET for the 1:00 PM appointment and is still waiting for a door. Free time ends at 3:00 PM ET. > **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] If the broker says “we don’t pay detention on this one”**
>
> **“Understood. The rate con lists detention after two hours at $50. Can you point me to where it says otherwise?”** If it truly isn’t there, note it for next time and add detention terms to your negotiation checklist.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Pakistan tip**
>
> Detention often starts in your early morning shift (U.S. afternoon). Set reminders at “free time minus 15 minutes” for every live appointment on your tracker.

## Lumper fees

*Lesson 10.1 · Lumpers*

Lesson 10.1 · Lumpers

# Lumper fees

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/lumper.jpg)

### Driver script at the receiver

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Carlos to the receiver**
>
> **“Before I pay anything, I need to call my dispatcher. Can I get the amount and who to make it out to?”** Then he calls you, and you call the broker.

> **[CHECKLIST] The lumper receipt must show**
>
> Facility name and address · date and time · amount · load or PO number · how it was paid · signature or stamp. A handwritten note on a napkin will not be reimbursed.

### Driver assist vs lumper

| Situation | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lumper** | Facility’s paid crew unloads | Broker pays or reimburses; get a receipt |
| **Driver assist** | Driver loads/unloads or counts by hand | Only if agreed in advance and paid; driver can refuse unsafe work |
| **No-touch** | Driver stays in the truck | Normal for most full truckload freight |

## The late ETA call

*Lesson 10.2 · Running late*

Lesson 10.2 · Running late

# The late ETA call

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/late.jpg)

### Drill 12: say it out loud

Read the call-first script above aloud three times. Then change the facts: **flat tyre at mile 42, repair 90 minutes**, and give a new ETA with the same structure: **load · what happened · new ETA · request · written follow-up**.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Never make up a reason**
>
> If the driver overslept or left late, say “the driver is running behind” and give an honest ETA. Brokers check tracking; invented traffic destroys trust.

> **[KEY RULE] Update again if it slips**
>
> If the new ETA changes by more than about 30 minutes, call again. One accurate update beats three hopeful ones.

### Quick ETA table (at a realistic 50 mph average)

| Miles left | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 | 250 | 300 | 400 | 500 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Driving time** | 1:00 | 2:00 | 3:00 | 4:00 | 5:00 | 6:00 | 8:00 | 10:00 |

Add fuel, traffic and check-in time, then check the driver’s hours (Module 4). Above about 500 miles, a 10-hour break usually falls in between.

## When the truck or the reefer fails

*Lesson 10.2 · Breakdowns and reefers*

Lesson 10.2 · Breakdowns and reefers

# When the truck or the reefer fails

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/breakdown.jpg)

#### Reefer problem checklist

Unit running? Setpoint matches the BOL? Continuous or cycle mode as required? Fuel level? Alarms? Download the temperature record. Tell the broker **before** the freight warms, and follow written instructions (Module 2).

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Recovery is the broker’s decision**
>
> If Truck 12 can’t be repaired in time, the broker decides whether to wait or send a recovery truck. Your carrier must not hand the load to another carrier on its own: that’s brokering (Module 8).

> **[FACT] Evidence for repairs**
>
> Keep the repair order, tow receipt and times. If the broker agrees to reschedule rather than cancel, get the new appointment in writing.

## Safety events

*Lesson 10.2 · Accidents and weather*

Lesson 10.2 · Accidents and weather

# Safety events

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/accident.jpg)

### Weather and hours

The driver decides whether it is safe to drive. FMCSA’s **adverse driving conditions** exception can allow up to 2 extra hours of driving (and extend the 14-hour window by up to 2 hours) when conditions such as snow, fog or unusual traffic weren’t known at dispatch. It is for finishing safely, not for pushing through a storm.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Coercion is prohibited**
>
> Carriers, shippers, receivers and brokers may not pressure a driver to break hours-of-service or other safety rules (49 CFR 390.6, Module 4). Never say “just push through.”

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] The weather call to the broker**
>
> **“Load PF-20931: heavy snow on I-75 north of Atlanta. The driver has stopped at a safe location. New ETA tomorrow 8 AM Eastern, weather permitting. I’ll update you by 6 AM.”**

## Cargo claims

*Lesson 10.3 · Claims*

Lesson 10.3 · Claims

# Cargo claims

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/claims.jpg)

### How claims reach the carrier

| Step | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Damage found | Receiver notes it on the POD; driver photographs |
| Claim filed | Shipper or its insurer, usually through the broker |
| Carrier response | Carrier, with its cargo insurer |
| Salvage | Damaged freight sold or disposed of to reduce the loss (49 CFR 370.11) |

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Payment offsets**
>
> Some brokers hold or deduct freight payment while a claim is open, depending on the broker–carrier agreement. Tell Mike, read the agreement, and respond with evidence. Don’t ignore the claim hoping it goes away.

> **[KEY RULE] Protect the carrier’s insurance**
>
> Tell the carrier the same day so it can notify its cargo insurer on time. Late notice can create problems with coverage.

## The evidence kit

*Lesson 10.3 · Evidence*

Lesson 10.3 · Evidence

# The evidence kit

![](/assets/diagrams/m10/evidence.jpg)

### Damage photo checklist for drivers

1. Wide shot of the trailer interior before unloading, if possible.
2. Close-ups of each damaged item and its label.
3. The seal (intact or broken) and the seal number.
4. The POD notation, readable.
5. Anything that shows cause: load bars, straps, shifting, wet floor.

> **[FACT] A notation is not a claim**
>
> Under 49 CFR 370.3, a note on a delivery receipt doesn’t count as a claim by itself. A claim is a written demand identifying the shipment, asserting liability and stating an amount. Notes and photos are the evidence for that claim.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Securement matters**
>
> Your M2 photos of load bars and blocked cargo show what prevents most shifting damage. Ask the driver to secure partial or light loads, and photograph it.

# Answers: situations and math

**S1.**

Free time runs 8:00–10:00 AM CT (arrived early, clock starts at the appointment). Send the detention notice now, before 10:00, with the check-in time. Ask Carlos to get in/out times on the BOL.

**S2.**

Carlos pays nothing yet. Call the broker: “Lumper $185 at the receiver; rate con says broker pays. How will you pay it?” Get a money code or direct payment; receipt with load #.

**S3.**

Photo of the gate/check-in, tracking arrival. Call the broker: cancellation in writing; invoice TONU $150 with proof. Tell Mike; start searching for a reload near Rockford.

**S4.**

Driver safe; roadside service. Call the broker: location, 3 h repair, reefer running at 34°F, new ETA. Temperature record downloaded. Broker decides on waiting or recovery.

**S5.**

Photos, exact POD note (21 received, 1 wet/damaged), seal intact and SLC on BOL. Call the broker before Carlos leaves; tell Mike the same day. Evidence supports that loading/counting was the shipper’s.

**S6.**

Safety first: no pushing. “The driver will stop at a safe place; with the snow and his hours he can’t deliver today. New ETA tomorrow 8 AM.” Tell Mike. Coercion is prohibited.

**7a.**

Clock 8:00 → out 12:05 = 4 h 05 min − 2 h = 2 h 05 → 8 full blocks = 2 h → **$100**.

**7b.**

1:00 → 4:40 = 3 h 40 − 2 h = 1 h 40 → 6 full blocks = 1 h 30 → **$75**.

**7c.**

Arrived after the appointment: detention void → **$0** (unless the broker agrees otherwise).

**7d.**

9:00 → 1:30 = 4 h 30 − 2 h = 2 h 30 → **$125**.

**8.**

Line haul $1,750.00 (rate con) · detention $75.00 (times on POD + notice email) · lumper $185.00 (receipt + broker approval) · **total $2,010.00**.

## Answers: module check

*Answer key*

Answer key

# Answers: module check

**1.**

Detention: paid waiting after free time. Layover: held overnight or a day. TONU: truck ordered, not used (cancelled after dispatch). Lumper: paid unloading crew.

**2.**

Usually at the appointment time (or arrival, if later). Arriving early is the carrier’s choice, not the facility’s delay.

**3.**

Terms on the rate con, times proven (tracking, BOL in/out), notice to the broker when required; then invoice with proof.

**4.**

A shipper or receiver that requires loading/unloading help must provide it or pay for it, and coercing a carrier to load or unload is unlawful.

**5.**

Driver calls you; you confirm with the broker who pays and how; payment method agreed; receipt obtained.

**6.**

Work out an honest ETA with hours, call the broker before the appointment, confirm in writing and ask for a new time.

**7.**

The broker (with its customer). Handing the load to another carrier without the broker arranging it is brokering.

**8.**

Setpoint on the BOL, unit download/temperature record, pulp temperatures, precool proof, times and messages.

**9.**

A fatality, an injury treated away from the scene, or a vehicle towed for disabling damage. Never admit fault, promise payment or guess.

**10.**

A notation records what was seen at delivery; a claim is a written demand identifying the shipment, asserting liability and stating an amount (49 CFR 370.3).

**11.**

At least 9 months to file a claim; at least 2 years to sue after written denial (49 U.S.C. 14706).

**12.**

Acknowledge within 30 days; pay, decline or offer within 120 days, with written status every 60 days if not resolved (49 CFR 370.5, 370.9).

## Glossary

# Glossary

**Accessorial** — Extra charge beyond the linehaul.

**Adverse driving conditions** — HOS exception adding up to 2 h when unexpected conditions arise.

**Carmack Amendment** — 49 U.S.C. 14706: carrier liability for cargo loss or damage.

**Claim** — Written demand with shipment, liability and amount.

**Detention** — Paid waiting after free time.

**Driver assist** — Driver helps load or unload, if agreed and paid.

**Free time** — Waiting allowed before detention starts (often 2 h).

**Layover** — Truck held overnight or to the next day.

**Lumper** — Paid unloading crew at a facility.

**Money code** — Payment code issued so a driver can pay a fee.

**Notice** — Message telling the broker free time is ending.

**Offset** — Broker holding payment against a claim.

**Out of service** — Vehicle or driver barred from moving until fixed.

**Recovery** — Moving freight after a breakdown, arranged by the broker.

**Salvage** — Selling damaged freight to reduce a loss.

**TONU** — Truck ordered, not used.
