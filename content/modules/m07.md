## Where the money goes

*Lesson 7.1 · Cost per mile*

Lesson 7.1 · Cost per mile

# Where the money goes

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/cost_stack.jpg)

### Cost per mile, in one line

**Cost per mile = all costs for a period ÷ all miles in that period**, loaded and empty. If Truck 12 spends $5,900 in a week and runs 2,500 miles, it costs **$2.36 a mile**.

Use **total miles**: an empty mile burns the same fuel and pays the same driver as a loaded one.

> **[FACT] ATRI benchmark (real data)**
>
> The American Transportation Research Institute surveys carriers every year. For 2025 it reported an average **$2.336 per mile**, a record, and **$1.854** without fuel. Driver wages were 81.8¢ and repairs 21.5¢ a mile. Use it to sanity-check a carrier’s numbers, not as their numbers.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Every carrier costs about $2 a mile”**
>
> Cost depends on the truck payment, the driver pay, insurance, fuel price and miles run. Two trucks on the same lane can differ by 50¢ a mile. Always use the carrier’s own numbers.

## Fuel: the swing cost

*Lesson 7.2 · Diesel*

Lesson 7.2 · Diesel

# Fuel: the swing cost

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/diesel.jpg)

### Fuel per mile

**Fuel cost per mile = diesel price ÷ mpg.** A heavy 53′ van usually gets about 6–7 mpg. Hills, speed, weight, weather and idling change it. The carrier knows its real number from fuel receipts and the ELD or fuel card.

Each **$1.00** rise in diesel adds about **15¢ a mile** at 6.5 mpg (1 ÷ 6.5 = $0.154). On 2,500 miles that’s about **$385 a week**.

> **[FACT] Where to check**
>
> **EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update**, published every week for the U.S. and by region. Week of 28 Sep 2026: **$6.382**, against **$3.754** a year earlier.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Pakistan tip**
>
> Put the EIA page in your Monday routine, just like the load board. When diesel moves more than about 25¢, tell the carrier and re-check the minimum rate together.

## How fuel gets paid

*Lesson 7.2 · Fuel surcharge*

Lesson 7.2 · Fuel surcharge

# How fuel gets paid

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/fsc.jpg)

### Worked example

A broker offers **“$1,400 linehaul plus fuel”** for 700 loaded miles and uses DAT’s method: ($6.382 − $1.25) ÷ 6.0 = $0.855 a mile.

Fuel = 700 × $0.855 = **$599**. All-in = $1,400 + $599 = **$1,999** = $2.86 per loaded mile. If deadhead is 50 miles: $1,999 ÷ 750 = **$2.67 per total mile**.

> **[KEY RULE] Always convert to all-in**
>
> Compare loads on the **all-in total ÷ total miles**. Two brokers can say “$1,400” and mean very different money.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Get it in writing**
>
> If the rate is “linehaul + FSC”, the rate confirmation must show how fuel is paid: a fixed amount, or the formula and the diesel price week. Otherwise the carrier may be paid only the linehaul.

### What a rate can include

| Part | Meaning | Module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Linehaul** | Pay for moving the load from A to B, without fuel | This module |
| **Fuel surcharge** | Extra pay that follows the diesel price | This module |
| **Accessorials** | Detention, layover, lumper, extra stops, TONU | Module 10 |
| **Deductions** | Money taken off for broken rules (tracking, late POD) | Next pages · Module 9 |

## Break-even and minimum rate

*Lesson 7.3 · Break-even*

Lesson 7.3 · Break-even

# Break-even and minimum rate

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/breakeven.jpg)

### Three numbers every dispatcher knows

| Number | Truck 12 |
| --- | --- |
| **Operating cost** | $2.36 per total mile |
| **Break-even** | $2.59: below this, every mile loses money |
| **Target minimum** | $2.70: break-even plus the carrier’s profit cushion |

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Why not just add 9%?**
>
> $2.36 + 9% = $2.57, which is too low. The fees are 9% of the **rate**, not of the cost. At $2.57 the fees are 23¢ and the carrier keeps $2.34, less than cost. Dividing by 0.91 fixes it.

> **[KEY RULE] The carrier sets the minimum**
>
> You calculate and explain. Mike decides his profit cushion and when to accept less for a good reason.

### Truck 12 break-even at different diesel prices and miles

| Diesel ($/gal) | Fuel / mi | 2,000 mi / week | 2,500 mi / week | 3,000 mi / week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $4.50 | $0.69 | $2.42 | $2.27 | $2.18 |
| $5.00 | $0.77 | $2.51 | $2.36 | $2.27 |
| $5.50 | $0.85 | $2.59 | $2.45 | $2.36 |
| $6.00 | $0.92 | $2.67 | $2.53 | $2.43 |
| **$6.38 (Sep 2026)** | **$0.98** | $2.74 | **$2.59** | $2.50 |
| $7.00 | $1.08 | $2.85 | $2.70 | $2.61 |

## Per loaded mile vs per total mile

*Lesson 7.3 · Using it*

Lesson 7.3 · Using it

# Per loaded mile vs per total mile

Load boards and rate tools show rates per **loaded** (trip) mile. Your break-even is per **total** mile. Convert before you compare:

**Rate per total mile** = all-in rate ÷ (deadhead + loaded miles)

**Load 1:** $2,050 ÷ 760 loaded = $2.70 “on the board”. With 40 deadhead: $2,050 ÷ 800 = **$2.56**. That’s 3¢ under break-even.

### The other direction: what should I ask for?

**Ask = target × total miles.** Load 1: $2.70 × 800 = **$2,160**. Walk-away (break-even): $2.59 × 800 = **$2,072**.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] The $2.40 story**
>
> Mike set $2.40 in spring, when diesel was about $5.00. Break-even then was **$2.36**, so $2.40 left 4¢. Diesel went to $6.38 and break-even became $2.59. Nobody updated the minimum, and Truck 12 started losing about 19¢ on every “minimum” mile.

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Tell the carrier early**
>
> **“Mike, with diesel at $6.38 our break-even on Truck 12 is about $2.59 a mile. Our $2.40 minimum now loses money. Can we set a new minimum around $2.70?”**

## Read the rate con for money

*Lesson 7.3 · Real document*

Lesson 7.3 · Real document

# Read the rate con for money

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/rc_annot.jpg)

> **[CHECKLIST] Money checklist for any rate con**
>
> 1. All-in total, or linehaul + fuel?
> 2. Miles shown: loaded only?
> 3. Accessorials listed (detention, lumper, TONU)?
> 4. Deductions and fines in the notes?
> 5. Payment terms: days, quick-pay fee, factor’s address?

> **[FACT] Fees that come off the top**
>
> **Factoring:** commonly about 1.5–3% (recourse) or 2.5–5% (non-recourse) per invoice, plus possible transfer and same-day fees (FreightWaves). **Dispatch:** often 5–10% of gross; 6–7% is common for vans (industry guides). Truck 12 uses 3% and 6%.

### Follow Load 1’s $2,050 to the carrier’s pocket

| Step | Amount | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate confirmation, all-in | $2,050.00 | Chicago → Charlotte, 760 loaded + 40 deadhead |
| − Factoring 3% | − $61.50 | Paid within a day or two instead of waiting for the broker |
| − Dispatch 6% | − $123.00 | Your employer’s fee (or your pay) |
| **= Carrier keeps** | **$1,865.50** | 91% of the rate |
| − Cost of 800 miles at $2.36 | − $1,888.00 | Fuel, driver, repairs + share of weekly fixed costs |
| **= Result** | **− $22.50** | A small loss: why you counter toward $2,160 |

## What is the lane really paying?

*Lesson 7.4 · Market rates*

Lesson 7.4 · Market rates

# What is the lane really paying?

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/rateview.jpg)

### How to read a lane range

- **Below the low end:** the posted rate is weak. Counter with confidence.
- **Around the average:** fair. Negotiate a little, decide on the whole trip.
- **Above the high end:** strong, or something is unusual (urgent load, hard pickup, fraud: check Module 8).

Rate tools usually need a paid subscription. Many carriers give their dispatcher access with the board seat.

> **[KEY RULE] Spot vs contract**
>
> **Spot** = one load, priced today, broker to carrier. **Contract** = rates agreed for months between shipper and carrier. You mostly work spot. DAT reports both, and they can move in different directions.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Don’t quote from memory**
>
> Rates change every week. Never tell a carrier “Chicago to Charlotte pays $2.60” from last month. Look it up again.

### No rate tool? Four other ways to judge a rate

| Method | How | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Compare similar posts** | Search the same lane and day; note posted rates on 3–5 comparable loads | Many loads post without a rate |
| **Your own history** | Your load economics cards for that lane over the last weeks | Old weeks may not match today |
| **Board market data** | Many boards show lane averages or rate insights beside the load | Check the date range and whether fuel is included |
| **Ask the broker** | “What’s your budget on this one?”, then counter from your numbers (Module 8) | Their first number is rarely their best |

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] What this means for Truck 12**
>
> A national van average of about $2.89 per loaded mile is only about **$2.51 per total mile** once you add 15% deadhead. That’s below Truck 12’s $2.59 break-even. In a soft market with expensive diesel, lane choice and negotiation decide whether the week makes money.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Averages hide lanes**
>
> Some lanes pay far above the national average and some far below. Use the national number to understand the market’s mood. Use lane tools for real decisions.

## The freight year

*Lesson 7.4 · Seasons*

Lesson 7.4 · Seasons

# The freight year

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/season.jpg)

### Questions seasons help you ask

- Is it produce season in the destination? (Reefers get scarce; van rates can rise too.)
- Is a holiday coming? (Plan pickups before docks close; expect a slow day after.)
- Is it quarter-end? (Shippers push volume in the last days.)
- Is weather coming? (Hurricane or winter storm: add time and ask about delays.)

> **[FACT] Roadcheck 2026**
>
> CVSA’s International Roadcheck ran **12–14 May 2026**: 72 hours of North American roadside inspections. Some drivers take those days off, so capacity can tighten briefly.

> **[KEY RULE] Weekly rhythm too**
>
> Mondays often bring fresh freight; Friday afternoons bring loads for Monday; weekends are thin in many markets. Watch your own board to learn your lanes.

## Per mile, per day, per week

*Lesson 7.5 · Rate per day*

Lesson 7.5 · Rate per day

# Per mile, per day, per week

![](/assets/diagrams/m07/perday.jpg)

### Rate per day

**Revenue per day = trip revenue ÷ days the truck is committed**, from dispatch until it is empty again and ready.

Truck 12 needs about **$6,500 a week** (2,500 mi × $2.59) to break even, which is about **$1,300 per working day** over five days.

| Plan | Margin vs break-even | Hours used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | −$144 | ~34 of 40 |
| B | −$498 | ~40 of 40 |
| C | +$35 and 8 h left for a short load | ~32 of 40 |

Margin = revenue − (total miles × $2.59).

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction: “Always take the highest rate per mile”**
>
> Short loads with high per-mile rates can pay less per day. Long loads into weak markets can look rich and still lose the week. Measure **per total mile**, **per day** and **where the truck ends up**.

> **[KEY RULE] Hours are money too**
>
> Plan B uses all 40 of Carlos’s remaining hours (Module 4). Plan C leaves room for one more short load before a 34-hour restart.

## Pitch the plan to the carrier

*Lesson 7.5 · Talking money*

Lesson 7.5 · Talking money

# Pitch the plan to the carrier

### What a good pitch contains

1. **The load:** lane, miles, pickup and delivery.
2. **The money:** offered rate, your counter, rate per total mile.
3. **The comparison:** against break-even and the market.
4. **The reason:** the next leg, days, hours, where the truck ends.
5. **The question:** “Do you want me to book it?”

> **[KEY RULE] The carrier decides**
>
> Never book below the carrier’s minimum without asking. Some carriers let you book above target on your own: get that permission in writing (Module 1 dispatch agreement).

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] Say it out loud · drill 9: pitching a plan**
>
> **“Mike, I have Bolingbrook to Atlanta, 715 miles, 12 empty, picks up Wednesday at 9, delivers Thursday at 1. The broker offered $1,600. I got them to $1,750, which is $2.41 a mile, under our $2.59 break-even by itself. But Atlanta is strong: I have a Thursday reload to Columbus for $1,700, so the two loads average $2.62, and Carlos is back near home Friday with hours left. Do you want me to book both?”**

> **[TRY IT] Your turn**
>
> Write and say the pitch for **Plan A**. Be honest that the trip averages $2.49, 10¢ under break-even, and suggest what you would try to improve it.

### Words that make money talk clear

| Say | Instead of |
| --- | --- |
| “$2.56 per total mile” | “good money” / “decent rate” |
| “3 cents under break-even” | “a bit low” |
| “$1,380 a day for 2.5 days” | “quick load” |
| “Atlanta shows many outbound loads today” | “Atlanta is always hot” |

## Glossary

*Reference*

Reference

# Glossary

**All-in rate** — One total that includes fuel.

**Break-even** — Rate per total mile where revenue after fees equals cost.

**Contract rate** — Rate agreed for months between shipper and carrier.

**Cost per mile** — All costs ÷ all miles in a period.

**Deduction** — Money taken off the pay for a broken rule.

**Dispatch fee** — Dispatcher’s pay, often a % of gross.

**EIA** — U.S. Energy Information Administration; publishes weekly diesel prices.

**Factoring** — Selling invoices for fast pay, for a fee (recourse / non-recourse).

**Fixed cost** — Paid every week regardless of miles.

**Fuel surcharge (FSC)** — Pay that follows the diesel price.

**Headhaul / backhaul** — Strong direction / weaker return direction.

**Linehaul** — Base rate without fuel.

**Load-to-truck ratio** — Load posts ÷ truck posts, per market and equipment.

**Market Conditions** — DAT’s hot/cold supply-demand map.

**Operating cost** — Fixed + variable cost per mile.

**Rate per day** — Trip revenue ÷ days the truck is committed.

**Rate per total mile** — All-in rate ÷ (deadhead + loaded).

**RateView** — DAT’s tool for recent paid lane rates.

**Spot rate** — One-off price for a single load.

**Target minimum** — Break-even plus the carrier’s profit cushion.

**Variable cost** — Grows with each mile.

**Walk-away rate** — Lowest rate you’d accept: usually break-even.
