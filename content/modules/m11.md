# How to use this module

This is the last handbook. The first half prepares **you**: your hours, your desk, your pay, your health and your first carrier. The second half tests **everything**: a five-day simulation with three trucks, where Claude plays the brokers, the driver and the carrier.

### Study rhythm (about 8 hours)

- **Session 1 (1.5 h):** time zones, setup, pay and tax, routine, first carrier.
- **Sessions 2–6 (1 h each):** one simulation day each, with that day’s role-plays.
- **Session 7 (1.5 h):** weekly report, capstone, final checklist.

### How the role-plays work

1. Read the day card and prepare your numbers.
2. Copy the start line from page 13 into the chat with Claude.
3. Talk (type or use voice) as you would on a real call.
4. Claude stays in character until the call ends, then scores you with the rubric (page 14) and shows a better version.
5. Repeat any role-play scoring below 7/10.

# A desk that doesn’t fail

![](/assets/diagrams/m11/setup.jpg)

### Start-of-shift checklist (10 minutes)

1. Power and backup internet working; headset tested.
2. Read the handover note; check every truck’s status.
3. EIA diesel (Mondays); any weather alerts on active lanes.
4. Load board, email, tracking and chat groups open.
5. Message the carrier: “Online and covering. Status: …”

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Use the carrier’s number, not your own**
>
> Brokers call back the numbers on the carrier’s FMCSA record and setup packet. Use the carrier’s U.S. business line (usually a softphone app); never give out a personal Pakistani number for business calls.

> **[SAY IT OUT LOUD] If your connection drops mid-call**
>
> Call back immediately from the backup: **“Sorry, I lost the line. This is Jawad from Blue Line again, about load PF-20931.”** Then tell the carrier if the outage continues.

### How carriers usually pay dispatchers

| Model | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Percentage** | A share of gross revenue (industry guides: often 5–10%; Module 7) |
| **Weekly flat fee** | Fixed amount per truck or per week |
| **Salary / hourly** | Employed or contracted by a dispatch company |

>

> **[FACT] Keep your own records**
>
> Agreement, invoices, bank advices and tax filings, stored safely. They prove your income source if your bank or FBR asks.

## A night-shift routine you can keep

*Lesson 11.1 · Health*

Lesson 11.1 · Health

# A night-shift routine you can keep

![](/assets/diagrams/m11/shift.jpg)

### Five habits of long-lasting night dispatchers

- Fixed sleep and wake times, seven days a week if possible.
- Light exercise and daylight before the shift.
- Water and real meals; limit caffeine in the last four hours of the shift.
- Short breaks every 90 minutes: stand, stretch, rest your eyes.
- A clear stop: handover note sent, laptop closed.

> **[KEY RULE] Coverage agreements**
>
> Agree in writing which hours you cover, who covers weekends and holidays, and how emergencies reach someone while you sleep.

> **[REAL-WORLD TIP] Pakistan realities**
>
> Plan around load-shedding schedules, Ramadan timings, and family events. Tell the carrier early about any day you can’t cover, just as you’d want to hear early about a late truck.

### What your dispatch agreement should cover (Module 1)

| Clause | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Services and hours** | What you do (search, book, track, paperwork) and when you are on duty |
| **Authority** | What you may book or sign without asking; the minimum rates |

| **Ending the agreement** | Notice period; handover of open loads and documents |

# Capstone model answer

**1.**

Fuel $6.70 ÷ 6.5 = $1.03. Cost = 0.52 + 1.03 + 0.86 = $2.41. Break-even = 2.41 ÷ 0.91 = **$2.65**. Target = (2.41 + 0.10) ÷ 0.91 = **$2.76**. The $2.70 minimum is now only 5¢ above break-even.

**2.**

Origin Memphis, DH-O 50 · destination Chicago area or zone covering IL (Z6) · dry van, full, 53 ft · 44,500 lb · pickup Tue.

**3.**

L1: ~540 mi ≈ 11 h driving + loading/unloading ≈ 14 h on duty: fits 38 h, delivers Wed morning, then ~45 mi to Joliet. Home long before Saturday, with hours left for a short Thursday/Friday load.

**4.**

L1 $1,550 ÷ 540 = **$2.87** (above $2.76) → BOOK after checks. L2 $2.65 = break-even, and ends 180 mi from home → pass. L3 $2.63 below break-even, slower-paying broker, but ends at home → only if L1 is gone; counter to ≥ $1,525.

**5.**

Check L&I: if a BMC-84 cancellation is pending, ask Harbor Point whether a replacement bond is filed. Don’t send the packet until it is resolved; send it only through their setup platform.

**6.**

“Mike: at $6.70 diesel break-even is $2.65, so I suggest a $2.76 minimum. For Truck 12 I’m calling on Memphis→Chicago, $1,550 ($2.87), pickup Tue 10 AM CT, Carlos home Wed with hours to spare. Harbor Point shows a bond cancellation pending, so I’m holding the packet until they confirm a replacement. OK to book L1?”
