# How to use this module

## USDOT, MC and authority status

*Lesson 5.1 · Identifiers*

Lesson 5.1 · Identifiers

# USDOT, MC and authority status

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/ids.jpg)

### How they appear in real life

- A broker on the phone: **“What’s your MC?”** You answer with the carrier’s docket number.
- A rate confirmation: carrier name, **MC and DOT** (remember load #12354’s RC).
- On the truck door: the **USDOT number** and company name.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Correction to older course notes**
>
> Some 2025 articles claimed “MC numbers are being eliminated.” FMCSA’s May 2026 Motus notice says docket numbers are **still issued**, the MC/MX/FF prefixes are unchanged, and **existing USDOT and docket numbers don’t change**. New registrants get a letter suffix on the USDOT number.

### What Motus changed (2025–26)

- New online registration system; all users verify identity with **Login.gov**, an anti-fraud step.
- **USDOT suffixes** for new registrants (C = property carrier, B = broker, and others). Not required on trucks.
- **One docket number per new authority**.
- A pending application now shows its **reason**, e.g. “Pending – Process Agent (Form BOC-3) Filings.”

## How an authority starts — and stays alive

*Lesson 5.1 · Authority life cycle*

Lesson 5.1 · Authority life cycle

# How an authority starts — and stays alive

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/timeline.jpg)

| Term | What it is | Why a dispatcher cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Common authority** | Authority to haul for the general public for hire (what most carriers have) | Must be ACTIVE to take broker freight |
| **BOC-3** | Filing that names process agents (people who accept legal papers) in each state | No BOC-3 → authority stays pending |
| **BIPD insurance filing** | The insurer files liability proof with FMCSA (BMC-91/91X); $750,000 minimum for general freight | Cancelled filing → authority can be revoked |
| **MCS-150** | Company census (trucks, drivers, operations); update at least every 2 years | Very old date can mean an inactive or careless carrier |
| **UCR** | Unified Carrier Registration: annual fee for interstate carriers | Unpaid UCR can mean fines at inspection |
| **IRP / IFTA** | Apportioned plates and fuel-tax reporting for trucks crossing states | Carrier’s job; affects which states the truck can run legally |

> **[FACT] “New authority” and brokers**
>
> Many brokers wait until an authority is about **90 days old** before giving loads, or give only lower-value freight at first. Others accept new carriers with extra checks. That is broker policy, not law, and it varies. When you start with a new carrier, **ask which big brokers have already approved them**.

## The carrier onboarding packet

*Lesson 5.2 · The packet*

Lesson 5.2 · The packet

# The carrier onboarding packet

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/packet.jpg)

### Day one with a new carrier

1. Sign the **dispatch agreement**.
2. Collect the six documents (PDF, clear, current).
3. Run the eight checks (page 18).
4. Fill in the carrier profile: trucks, trailers, equipment, lanes, home time, floor rate, contacts.
5. Ask which brokers already have the carrier set up.

> **[KEY RULE] One clean PDF packet**
>
> Save one combined, clearly named file: **“BlueLine\_Packet\_2026-09.pdf”** (MC cert + W-9 + COI + NOA or bank letter). Brokers love fast, clean packets, and it shows the carrier is professional.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Protect the documents**
>
> A packet contains tax IDs and bank details. Fraudsters use stolen packets to impersonate carriers. Send it only to verified brokers, through their official setup link or verified email (Module 8).

## Insurance types a dispatcher must understand

*Lesson 5.2 · Insurance*

Lesson 5.2 · Insurance

# Insurance types a dispatcher must understand

| Coverage | What it covers | Law / typical broker ask | Dispatcher check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Auto liability (BIPD)** | Injury and damage the truck causes to others | FMCSA min. $750,000 general freight · brokers usually $1,000,000 | Limit, dates, truck covered (scheduled autos) |
| **Motor truck cargo** | Damage to or loss of the freight being hauled | Not federally required for general freight · brokers often ≥ $100,000 | Limit vs load value; exclusions (reefer breakdown, electronics, theft) |
| **General liability** | Non-driving incidents, e.g. damage at a customer’s dock | Some brokers / shippers ask ($1M) | Present if the broker requires it |
| **Physical damage** | The carrier’s own truck and trailer | Carrier’s choice / lender | Rarely asked by brokers |
| **Non-owned trailer / interchange** | Trailers the carrier pulls but doesn’t own | Needed for power-only and drop trailers | Present before any power-only load (Module 2) |
| **Workers’ compensation** | Employee injuries (state rules) | Some brokers ask, or an exemption form | Owner-operators may be exempt |

> **[KEY RULE] What the sample COI teaches**
>
> • **Auto $1M, cargo $250K**: meets most broker norms.  
> • **Scheduled autos**: only listed trucks are covered, so ask for the schedule when a new truck is added.  
> • **Non-owned trailer $50K**: OK for power only up to that value.  
> • **Reefer breakdown included**: important for reefer loads.  
> • **Insured blank and dates expired**: this copy would be rejected.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] A COI is not a guarantee**
>
> ACORD 25 says it is “issued as a matter of information only and confers no rights upon the certificate holder.” Policies can be cancelled after the COI is printed. That is why brokers **call the agent** or use monitoring services, and why you re-check before new setups.

> **[FACT] Certificate holder requests**
>
> Each broker wants its name in the certificate-holder box. You email the carrier’s insurance agent: *“Please issue a COI to ABC Logistics, [address], certificate holder.”* Agents usually send it straight to the broker.

## Factoring and the Notice of Assignment

*Lesson 5.2 · Factoring*

Lesson 5.2 · Factoring

# Factoring and the Notice of Assignment

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/noa_annot.jpg)

### How factoring works

The carrier sells its invoices to a factoring company, which pays the carrier within a day or two, minus a fee, then collects from the broker when payment is due. The **NOA** legally tells the broker: “pay the factor, not the carrier.”

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Why it matters to you**
>
> If the broker pays the carrier directly after receiving an NOA, the factor can make the broker **pay twice**. So brokers take NOAs seriously. If your carrier changes factoring companies, the old factor must send a **release letter** before the new NOA takes effect. Keep both on file.

## Bank details and the dispatch agreement

*Lesson 5.2 · Payment details & your agreement*

Lesson 5.2 · Payment details & your agreement

# Bank details and the dispatch agreement

#### Voided check (sample from your manual)

Shows the **routing number** (bank, 9 digits) and **account number** so a broker can pay by ACH. Many carriers send a **bank letter** instead. Not needed if the carrier factors: the NOA replaces it.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Bank-change fraud**
>
> A classic scam: an email “from the carrier” asks the broker to change the bank account. Brokers verify changes by phone with a known contact. If your carrier changes banks, **tell brokers by phone as well as email**.

### Spot the error on the sample

The printed check number is **3256**, but the label under it says “Cheque Number **5337**.” It’s a small thing on a teaching picture, but it is exactly the kind of mismatch you must notice on real documents.

### The dispatch agreement

The written relationship between you (or your employer) and the carrier. It supports the **carrier-agent** position from Module 1: you act for one carrier, paid by that carrier, and you never handle broker money. The next page shows what it should cover.

> **[KEY RULE] Authority to sign**
>
> Brokers often ask the person completing setup: “Are you authorised to sign for the carrier?” Only say yes if your dispatch agreement says so. Otherwise send the agreement to the owner for signature.

## Broker carrier setup

Lesson 5.3 · Broker setup

# Broker carrier setup

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/flow.jpg)

### What the broker sees

Setup platforms pull FMCSA data automatically: authority, insurance filings, safety data, sometimes identity signals and monitoring alerts. The broker adds its own rules: authority age, minimum insurance, safety thresholds, factoring company, sometimes a phone call to the carrier using the number on FMCSA records.

> **[KEY RULE] Speed tips**
>
> • Keep the carrier’s profile complete on the big platforms.  
> • Have the insurance agent’s email ready for certificate-holder requests.  
> • Answer the broker’s verification call quickly, from a number they can match.  
> • Start setup **before** negotiating hard on a new broker’s load if it is time-sensitive.

## The broker’s carrier profile form

*Lesson 5.3 · Broker setup*

Lesson 5.3 · Broker setup

# The broker’s carrier profile form

**Real carrier profile form** from your OMNITECH manual (a broker called Metro Express): legal name, contacts, states served, services, equipment by type, references.

### Fill it in from your carrier notes

- **Legal name** exactly as on the MC certificate and W-9.
- **Dispatch day and night phone**: your number and the owner’s. Make sure you can answer.
- **States served**: the carrier’s real lanes. Over-ticking every state gets you spam.
- **Equipment**: exact types (53′ van, 26′ box truck with liftgate…) from Module 2.
- **Special requirements**: hazmat, team, pallet exchange. Tick only what is true.
- **References**: brokers the carrier already hauls for.

> **[WARNING / CORRECTION] Never exaggerate capabilities**
>
> Ticking “hazmat” or “reefer” for a carrier that isn’t qualified gets loads offered that you can’t legally haul, and can void insurance coverage on a claim.

### Other things brokers may ask

Broker–carrier agreement signature, payment terms or quick-pay option, factoring details, ELD provider and tracking consent, safety or accident history, and a recently signed W-9.

Lesson 5.4 · Verification

# Verifying your own carrier

![](/assets/diagrams/m05/verify.jpg)

### Why check your own carrier?

Because the broker will. If insurance has lapsed or the authority is inactive, the setup fails, or worse, a load moves without valid coverage. Catching it first protects your carrier, your reputation and the load.

> **[KEY RULE] Repeat the live checks**
>
> SAFER and L&I status and COI dates can change any day. Re-check them **monthly**, **before each new broker setup**, and **whenever the carrier mentions insurance changes**.

### L&I screen (page 8)

No: broker authority is NONE. $1,000,000 BIPD is on file, above the $750,000 federal minimum.

### Lookup results (page 8)

| # | Conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Cannot haul for hire across state lines. Stop. |

### Packet audit (page 20) — problems

1. **Names don’t match:** MC certificate = ABC Company LLC; W-9 = Fantastic Tacos, LLC; NOA = Amagination Reaality, LLC. Three different companies!
2. **COI “Insured” box is blank**: no carrier named.
3. **COI expired**: policy period ended 04/01/2023.
4. **COI certificate holder is blank**: the broker would need its own certificate.
5. **W-9 is an old revision** (Oct 2018); the IRS’s current version is March 2024. The address (Phoenix) doesn’t match the MC certificate (Jacksonville).
6. **NOA MC number (1406468) differs** from the certificate (MC-1395526).
7. **Voided check**: the account holder is a person (John Doe), not the company; the cheque number label doesn’t match. And if the carrier factors (NOA), payment goes to the factor, so the check shouldn’t be used.

> **[KEY RULE] Lesson**
>
> Each document looks fine alone. Together they are clearly not one carrier. **Always cross-check names, numbers, addresses and dates across the whole packet.**

## Answers — module check

Answer key

# Answers — module check

**1.**

USDOT = identity and safety record; MC = operating authority to haul for hire; status = whether that authority is active today.

**2.**

No. Docket numbers are still issued and existing numbers don’t change. Motus added USDOT suffixes for new registrants, Login.gov identity checks, one docket per new authority, and pending-reason displays.

**3.**

SAFER: identity, size, inspections, crashes, rating, operating status. L&I: authority types and statuses, insurance filings, BOC-3.

**4.**

$750,000 BIPD for general freight; brokers usually want $1,000,000 auto liability and often ≥ $100,000 cargo.

**5.**

A COI is information only; the policy can be cancelled after printing, or the truck may not be on it. Verify with the agent.

**6.**

Only the trucks listed on the policy are covered. The dispatched truck must be on the schedule.

**7.**

Pay the factoring company, not the carrier. If ignored, the broker may have to pay twice.

**8.**

Any four: parties; services; authority to sign; compensation paid by the carrier only; carrier decides on loads; no brokering; payments go directly to carrier or factor; term; confidentiality.

**9.**

Any five: names don’t match; expired COI; insured blank; truck not on policy; cargo exclusions; authority inactive; contact mismatch; surprise NOA; bank-change request by email.

**10.**

Packets hold tax IDs and bank details. Fraudsters use them to impersonate carriers and redirect payments.

## Glossary

*Reference*

Reference

# Glossary

**ACORD 25** — Standard certificate-of-insurance form.

**Authority** — FMCSA permission to operate for hire (common/contract/broker).

**BIPD** — Bodily injury and property damage (auto liability) insurance.

**BMC-91 / 91X** — Insurer’s liability filing with FMCSA.

**BOC-3** — Filing naming process agents in each state.

**Cargo insurance** — Covers the freight being hauled.

**Certificate holder** — Party (e.g. a broker) named on a COI.

**COI** — Certificate of insurance.

**Dispatch agreement** — Contract between the carrier and its dispatcher.

**Docket number** — MC / MX / FF authority number.

**EIN** — Employer Identification Number (tax ID).

**Factoring** — Selling invoices to a factor for fast cash.

**L&I** — FMCSA Licensing & Insurance records.

**Login.gov** — Federal identity-verification service used by Motus.

**MCS-150** — Carrier census form; update every 2 years.

**Motus** — FMCSA’s registration system (2025–26).

**NOA** — Notice of assignment: pay the factor.

**Non-owned trailer** — Insurance for trailers the carrier pulls but doesn’t own.

**Out-of-service** — Order barring a company, driver or vehicle from operating.

**SAFER** — FMCSA public company snapshot lookup.

**Scheduled autos** — Policy covers only listed vehicles.

**SMS** — FMCSA Safety Measurement System.

**USDOT number** — FMCSA company identifier.

**USDOT suffix** — Letter after a new USDOT number showing entity type.

**W-9** — IRS form giving a taxpayer ID to a payer.
